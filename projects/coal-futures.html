Precarious futures in Australian coal mining towns

Coal miners, pro-coal lobbyists and the everyday making of the climate crisis — the research behind Coal's Final Void.

Years
2014–2019
Role
Sole researcher
Institution
London School of Economics (PhD, 2019)
Supervisors
Gisa Weszkalnys and Deborah James

Kari's doctoral research involved two years of in-depth participant observation in Australian coal mining communities. Rather than framing coal communities or lobbyists as either victims or villains, it explores how moral claims are mobilised in everyday life and how these shape energy futures.

The work contributes to debates on the Anthropocene and energy ethics, showing how climate change is lived not as an abstract crisis but as an ethical and moral problem entangled with people's livelihoods, identities and futures. By recognising how accusations and counter-accusations entrench polarisation, it points towards ways of engaging fossil-fuel communities that move beyond moralising.

Coal’s Final Void: Extracted Futures and the Everyday Making of the Climate Crisis
Routledge, 2025

The book based on this research.
