Whyalla Futures

A research project listening to people in Whyalla about steel, hydrogen, work and what the future of the city could be.

Why Whyalla

Whyalla has been built around heavy industry for generations. Today it sits at the centre of some of Australia’s biggest promises about the energy transition. Those promises include greener steel, a hydrogen hub, and new jobs and investment for the Upper Spencer Gulf.

I am an anthropologist, and my work looks at how communities live with big, uncertain plans for their future. In Whyalla, hydrogen and steel futures have been announced, revised and put on hold, sometimes several times over. People who live and work here have watched those plans change, and they know things about them that reports and media releases miss.

That is why I want to spend time in Whyalla. The aim is not only to study the city. It is to understand what these changes mean for the people expected to host them, and to make sure local experiences and hopes are part of the conversation about Australia’s industrial future.

What the research asks

  • How do plans to make Whyalla a hydrogen “hub” shape local expectations of jobs, growth and a cleaner future?
  • How do today’s plans connect to Whyalla’s longer history of industrial change?
  • What do people in Whyalla want from the energy transition, and what worries them?
  • When do big future plans speed up change, and when do they hold it back?

Whyalla Futures is part of Hydrogen Hub Futures, a project at Monash University funded by the Australian Research Council (2025–2029).

Collaborations

This research is done with people and organisations in Whyalla, not just about them.

A local community collective. Kari works with the Whyalla Collective as part of Hydrogen Hub Futures, including on Voices of Whyalla.

Monash University

The research is based in the School of Social Sciences at Monash University and follows Monash University’s research ethics requirements.
