Whyalla is where the challenge of decarbonising heavy industry meets a city that's heard many promises. What futures do people here want, for themselves and their community?

Voices from Whyalla

Whyalla Futures Research is led by Dr Kari Dahlgren, Senior Lecturer in Anthropology at Monash University. It is part of Hydrogen Hub Futures, an Australian Research Council Discovery Early Career Researcher Award (DECRA) project (DE250100024, 2025–2029).

Research questions

This project uses ethnography, futures methods and co-design workshops to understand how people in Whyalla, South Australia, imagine their futures. The path to decarbonising iron and steel making is uncertain, and the people who live with that uncertainty deserve a say in what comes next.

RQ1

Who imagines Whyalla's future?

How are futures for the city produced by governments, companies and investors, and what models, like the hydrogen hub, do they rely on?

RQ2

How are promises lived?

How do residents experience repeated cycles of announcement, hope, delay and insecurity, and how does that history shape trust in the next plan?

RQ3

What does decarbonising steel mean here?

What are the everyday stakes of industrial decarbonisation for jobs, health, landscape and identity in a steel city?

RQ4

Whose futures are missing?

Whose voices and desires are absent from top-down approaches to planning regional industrial cities, and what do they imagine instead?

    Researcher

    Dr Kari Dahlgren

    Senior Lecturer in Anthropology · Monash University

    Dr Kari Dahlgren is a social anthropologist and ethnographer whose research examines the social and ethical dimensions of energy production, consumption, and transformation in Australia.

    Her research is broadly concerned with how futures are anticipated and negotiated, and with the uneven effects that follow.
